Selena Mangione Media's Impact

How Stories Shape Perception

True crime stories have always fascinated people, but how they’re told has changed over time. The way the media reports on crime decides who gets remembered: the victim, the accused, or the larger story behind the case. By looking at the cases of Selena Quintanilla, Brian Thompson, and Luigi Mangione, we can see how public perception is shaped by what the media chooses to focus on. Selena Quintanilla’s tragic death brought the world together to mourn her talent and impact on Latino culture. Meanwhile, the story of Brian Thompson’s death has become less about him and more about Luigi Mangione, his accused killer. These two cases highlight how media bias and sensationalism can shift the spotlight from the victim to the accused. From streaming platforms to TikTok debates, crime stories are no longer just about justice—they’re about engagement, views, and clicks. This raises important questions: Why do some victims become icons while others fade into the background? Why does the accused sometimes take center stage? And how does the media’s focus affect the way people think about crime, justice, and morality? Through these cases, we’ll explore how the media creates heroes, villains, and legends. We’ll also consider the role of social media, streaming platforms, and public opinion in shaping modern crime stories. These questions don’t have simple answers, but they show how powerful storytelling can be—and how it impacts the way we remember history.
