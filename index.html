Welcome to Crime, Media, and Morality In today’s digital age, crime isn’t just reported—it’s shaped, dissected, and turned into entertainment. From headline news to Netflix documentaries, viral TikTok debates to YouTube deep dives, the way we consume crime stories has fundamentally changed. But with this shift comes important questions: Who does the media choose to focus on? Why do some victims become immortalized while others fade into the background? And how does public perception shape justice, sympathy, and even the legacy of a crime? At Crime, Media, and Morality, we explore the ethical dilemmas of crime storytelling. We analyze media bias, true crime trends, and the growing influence of social media in shaping narratives. Whether it’s the undying support for Selena Quintanilla or the TikTok-driven rise of figures like Luigi Mangione, we dive into how public opinion transforms criminals, victims, and cases into something more than just facts. Crime is more than a headline—it’s a story with real consequences. Let’s question how it’s told.

The Queen of Tejano Music

A Legacy Cut Short Selena Quintanilla, recognized as the “Queen of Tejano Music” was an icon in the Latino community throughout her singing career. Her influence caused a great shift in music, which prompted many to recognize her as a cultural symbol of success, hope, and ambition. However, on March 31, 1995, Selena was murdered by Yolanda Saldivar, the former president of her fan club and associate. The Rise of a Superstar Born on April 16, 1971, in Lake Jackson, Texas, Selena was destined for greatness. Under the guidance of her father, Abraham Quintanilla, she began singing in her family's band, Selena y Los Dinos. Her unique voice, captivating performances, and charming personality quickly brought her to fame. By the early 1990s, she had become a household name, winning Grammy Awards, breaking records, and even expanding into the fashion industry. With dreams of crossing over into the English-language music market, Selena was set to become an international superstar. The Betrayal by Yolanda Saldívar Yolanda Saldívar at first seemed like a devoted fan who deeply admired Selena. In 1991, she founded Selena’s fan club and later was able to be in charge of managing the singer’s boutiques. However, behind the scenes, Saldívar was embezzling thousands of dollars from both the fan club and the boutiques. When suspicions arose, Selena and her family confronted her, leading to her eventual dismissal from the company. Despite her leave, Selena agreed to meet with Saldívar at the Days Inn motel in Corpus Christi, Texas, on the morning of March 31, 1995. Selena was there to retrieve financial documents, unaware of the danger that awaited her. In a shocking act of betrayal, Saldívar pulled out a gun and shot Selena in the back as she attempted to escape. The singer, bleeding and in distress, managed to run to the motel lobby, where she identified Saldívar as her shooter before collapsing. She was rushed to the hospital but succumbed to her injuries at just 23 years old. The Aftermath and Trial Selena’s death sent shockwaves through the world, devastating her family, friends, and millions of fans. Several vigils were held in her honor, and her funeral was attended by thousands. The trial of Yolanda Saldívar began in October 1995. She claimed the shooting was accidental, but the evidence suggested otherwise. After a high-profile case, Saldívar was convicted of first-degree murder and sentenced to life in prison with the possibility of parole after 30 years. Selena’s Lasting Legacy Despite her untimely death, Selena’s influence continues to thrive. Her music remains timeless, inspiring new generations of fans. The 1997 biographical film Selena, starring Jennifer Lopez, introduced her story to an even broader audience. Additionally, the Netflix series Selena: The Series further explored her life and career. Her posthumous album Dreaming of You, released after her death, debuted at number one on the Billboard 200, solidifying her status as a crossover legend. Selena’s story is one of talent, perseverance, and tragedy. While her life was taken far too soon, her impact on the music industry and Latino culture is everlasting. Her legacy lives on through her music, fashion, and the hearts of those who continue to celebrate her life.

Deny, Defend, Depose

The Luigi Mangione Case: When the Alleged Killer Becomes the Story In stark contrast to how the media framed the case of Selena Quintanilla, the coverage of Luigi Mangione has focused overwhelmingly on him rather than the man he is accused of murdering. While the victim, Brian Thompson, was a powerful figure in the healthcare industry, the media's fixation has been on Mangione—his past, his motives, and the movement that has emerged around him. Who Was Brian Thompson? Despite being the victim, Brian Thompson has not been the primary focus of media coverage. As the CEO of UnitedHealthcare, he led one of the largest insurance companies in the U.S., playing a significant role in the country's healthcare system. Yet, his career, contributions, and even his family’s response to his death have largely been overshadowed. The media’s treatment of Thompson has been strikingly minimal. Instead of in-depth retrospectives on his life and work, the conversation has been dominated by discussions about his accused killer. This has shifted public discourse from the tragedy of his death to the motivations and background of Luigi Mangione. Luigi Mangione: The Anti-Hero or Criminal? Luigi Mangione, a former engineer with chronic health conditions, had long expressed frustration with the American healthcare system. When he was arrested, police found a 3D-printed gun and a note criticizing insurance companies. These details quickly shifted media attention from Thompson’s murder to Mangione’s grievances, with many portraying him as either a cold-blooded criminal or a vigilante fighting against corporate greed. Online communities have only intensified this shift in narrative. Reddit forums, crowdfunding campaigns, and social media movements have cast Mangione as a symbol of rebellion against an unjust healthcare system. A fundraising campaign for his legal defense has raised hundreds of thousands of dollars, with supporters viewing him as a man pushed to the brink by corporate exploitation. The Role of Media Sensationalism Rather than centering the discussion on Thompson’s life and the impact of his murder, the media has largely sensationalized Mangione’s personal history. Stories about his education, medical struggles, and alleged motivations have dominated headlines, sometimes even straying into the bizarre—such as rumors about explicit videos featuring him. These rumors, rather than detracting from his support, have only fueled further donations to his defense fund, as supporters dismiss them as smear tactics. The case of Luigi Mangione highlights a broader pattern in media coverage: when a crime captures public attention, the alleged perpetrator often becomes the focal point rather than the victim. Whether this is due to public fascination with anti-establishment figures or a systemic bias in reporting, the result is the same—the person who lost their life fades into the background. A Case That Exposes a Broken System As of March 2025, Mangione remains incarcerated, awaiting trial. His supporters continue to rally around him, viewing his actions as a symptom of a broken system rather than an isolated crime. Meanwhile, Brian Thompson’s legacy remains largely unexplored, a footnote in a story that has become more about the accused than the victim. The Luigi Mangione case serves as a striking example of how the media can shape public perception, sometimes shifting focus away from those who have lost their lives and onto the figures who capture public imagination. It forces us to ask: who gets to be remembered in the court of public opinion, and at what cost?

Media's Impact

The Media's Role in Shaping Crime Narratives: Who Becomes the Story? In the age of endless streaming documentaries, viral TikTok movements, and sensationalized news coverage, the way we consume crime has fundamentally changed. The media plays a crucial role in shaping public perception, often determining who is the focal point of a case—whether it’s the victim, the perpetrator, or the broader narrative surrounding the crime. Two cases, decades apart, illustrate this: the murder of Selena Quintanilla and the assassination of Brian Thompson. In one, the world mourned the victim; in the other, attention has largely shifted to the accused. This contrast exposes a larger conversation about media bias, the morality of crime storytelling, and the increasing influence of social media in shaping public opinion. Who the Media Chooses to Focus On In the case of Selena Quintanilla, the media rightfully fixated on the tragedy of her death, her legacy, and the impact she had on music and culture. Yolanda Saldívar, the woman who pulled the trigger, was widely vilified, her image forever tied to betrayal and murder. Few people sympathized with her, and her motives—though reported—were secondary to the loss of Selena. The world was united in grief, and media coverage reflected that sentiment. Fast forward to 2024, and the assassination of Brian Thompson presents a starkly different narrative. Unlike Selena, Thompson—despite being the victim—has been largely ignored in public discourse. Instead, the accused killer, Luigi Mangione, has taken center stage. Media outlets have delved deep into his life, his health struggles, and his grievances against the healthcare system, painting him as either a misguided vigilante or a cold-blooded murderer. The victim’s personal history, contributions, and even his family’s response have been drowned out by discussions about Mangione’s ideology and the social movement that has erupted around him. Why Does This Happen? The way the media constructs crime narratives depends heavily on public sentiment, cultural context, and who they feel is “in the right.” In Selena’s case, there was no ambiguity—her talent and kindness made her an indisputable victim, and her death was a collective loss. But in the case of Brian Thompson, the narrative is more complicated. Because he was the CEO of a massive insurance company—a sector widely criticized for its role in American healthcare—some people view his death through a lens of systemic frustration. Mangione, with his health struggles and alleged manifesto, has been positioned as a product of that broken system rather than just a criminal. This shift in focus is partly due to how the public now consumes crime stories. True crime documentaries, Hulu and Netflix specials, and TikTok deep dives have made audiences more invested in the psychology of the perpetrator than the story of the victim. People want to know why a person committed a crime, what drove them to that moment, and whether they were justified in their actions. This shift in storytelling prioritizes the accused over those they harmed. According to a study by Pennington & Brown (2021), media consumers are more likely to engage with narratives that focus on the motivations and backgrounds of criminals rather than the lives of victims. This engagement leads to higher viewership and more in-depth storytelling around the perpetrators rather than those affected by their actions (Pennington & Brown, 2021). The Morality of Crime Storytelling This phenomenon raises ethical questions about how crime is reported and consumed. By turning criminals into characters with backstories, motives, and even fan bases, the media risks overshadowing the real impact of their crimes. Is it ethical to turn a killer into a folk hero if their grievances resonate with the public? Does media attention validate certain crimes while erasing the suffering of victims? With the rise of social media, public sentiment is no longer just shaped by traditional media but also by platforms like TikTok, where narratives are created and spread rapidly. Luigi Mangione has become a viral figure, with thousands of TikTok videos analyzing his case, debating his motives, and even advocating for his release. Meanwhile, Brian Thompson has been reduced to a name in headlines. The same way Selena’s supporters keep her memory alive decades after her death, Mangione’s growing supporters are ensuring his story remains at the forefront—though for very different reasons. Media analysts such as Jones & Carter (2023) argue that this trend is dangerous, as it shifts public perception of crime from one of accountability to one of selective justification. When people believe that a criminal’s motivations outweigh the crime itself, the justice system’s role in maintaining impartiality is challenged (Jones & Carter, 2023). The Influence of Social Media and Streaming Platforms Streaming platforms and YouTube creators have transformed crime stories into entertainment. Every major case now spawns multiple documentaries, reenactments, and exposés. While this helps bring attention to injustices, it also blurs the lines between awareness and sensationalism. Social media takes this even further, giving rise to movements, hashtags, and fandoms around alleged criminals. It’s no longer just about justice; it’s about engagement, content, and clicks. The Luigi Mangione case is a direct reflection of this. While traditional media is dissecting his motives, social media has turned him into a symbol of resistance against corporate greed. Meanwhile, his alleged crime and its consequences fade into the background. As McNamara (2024) states in The Crime Effect, "We are in an era where justice is not just served in courts but debated in the comment sections, determined by algorithms, and repackaged for mass consumption." The morality of true crime entertainment is now as much a topic of discussion as the crimes themselves. Final Thoughts: Who Deserves the Spotlight? The contrast between Selena Quintanilla’s case and Luigi Mangione’s illustrates how media bias dictates who the public remembers. Some victims are immortalized, while others are forgotten in favor of a more compelling narrative. The rise of social media has only intensified this effect, allowing public opinion to shape crime stories in real-time. As true crime continues to dominate pop culture, we must ask ourselves: Are we consuming these stories responsibly? Are we giving victims the attention they deserve, or are we more interested in turning criminals into legends? The morality of crime storytelling is murky, but one thing remains clear—the media determines whose story is worth telling, and that decision shapes the way history remembers crime.
