The Media’s Role in Crime Stories: Who Becomes the Focus?

These days, with streaming documentaries, viral TikToks, and dramatic news coverage, the way we see crime has totally changed. The media plays a big role in shaping how the public thinks about crime. They decide who gets the most attention—the victim, the criminal, or the story itself. Two cases show how this plays out: the murder of Selena Quintanilla and the death of Brian Thompson. One focused on the victim; the other turned into a story about the accused. This shows how the media can shift the way people see things.

Who the Media Chooses to Focus On

When Selena Quintanilla was killed, the media focused on her tragic death, her accomplishments, and her influence on music and culture. Her killer, Yolanda Saldívar, was seen as a villain. People didn’t sympathize with Yolanda, and her motives didn’t matter as much as the loss of Selena. The world was heartbroken, and the media reflected that. But in Brian Thompson’s case, it’s different. Even though he’s the victim, the media barely talks about him. Instead, they focus on Luigi Mangione, the man accused of killing him. They talk about Mangione’s health issues, his frustrations with the healthcare system, and his reasons for the crime. Meanwhile, Brian’s life, career, and even his family’s pain have been ignored. The accused killer has taken over the story.

TWhy Does This Happen?

The way the media tells crime stories depends on what the public feels and the cultural moment. Selena’s case was clear—she was a beloved icon, and her death was a huge loss. But Brian’s case is more complicated. As a CEO of a big insurance company, some people see him as part of a system they don’t like. Mangione, with his personal struggles and angry message, is seen by some as a victim of that system rather than just a criminal. This change is also because people now consume crime stories differently. True crime shows on Netflix and TikTok deep dives focus more on the accused than the victim. Viewers want to know why a person committed a crime and what pushed them to do it. This kind of storytelling puts the spotlight on the criminal instead of the person they hurt.

The Problem with Crime Stories

This trend raises important questions. Is it right for the media to make criminals into heroes if people relate to their motives? Does it take away from the victims and their families? With social media, these questions become even harder to answer. TikTok videos about Mangione have gone viral, with people defending him and demanding justice for his actions. At the same time, Brian Thompson’s name is barely mentioned. While Selena’s fans keep her memory alive, Mangione’s supporters make sure he stays in the spotlight—for very different reasons.

Social Media’s Influence

Streaming platforms and social media have turned crime stories into entertainment. Big cases now have documentaries, reenactments, and debates online. Social media pushes this further, creating movements and hashtags for alleged criminals. Justice becomes less about the courts and more about likes, shares, and comments. Mangione’s case shows this shift clearly. Traditional media talks about his motives, but social media has turned him into a symbol of resistance. At the same time, Brian’s death fades into the background.

Final Thoughts

The difference between Selena’s case and Mangione’s shows how the media decides who people remember. Some victims are honored, while others are forgotten because the accused seems like a better story. As true crime grows more popular, it’s important to ask if we’re focusing on the right things. Are we honoring victims or turning criminals into legends? The way the media tells these stories shapes how we remember them, and that choice matters.
