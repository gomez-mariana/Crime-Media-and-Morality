Media's Impact

The Media's Role in Shaping Crime Narratives: Who Becomes the Story?

In the age of endless streaming documentaries, viral TikTok movements, and sensationalized news coverage, the way we consume crime has fundamentally changed. The media plays a crucial role in shaping public perception, often determining who is the focal point of a case—whether it’s the victim, the perpetrator, or the broader narrative surrounding the crime. This evolution raises ethical concerns about storytelling, justice, and accountability.

Who the Media Chooses to Focus On

The case of Selena Quintanilla contrasted with that of Brian Thompson highlights media bias. In Selena’s story, the victim was celebrated; in Thompson’s case, the alleged perpetrator, Luigi Mangione, has taken the spotlight, leaving the victim overshadowed. Such disparities shape how society remembers crime.

The Morality of Crime Storytelling

True crime's popularity has blurred lines between awareness and sensationalism. While some cases garner compassion for victims, others become more about the accused's narrative. This shift raises questions about justice and the ethics of storytelling, amplified by social media's influence.

Final Thoughts

The contrast between these cases showcases how media determines whose story gets told and remembered. As consumers of true crime, we must reflect on whether we’re consuming responsibly or contributing to imbalances in public perception.
